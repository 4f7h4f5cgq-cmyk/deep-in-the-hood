Yesterday: a Smokey Robinson or Miracles song. The block said Tracks of My Tears and Cruisin'. Grandma's Favorite? Quiet Storm, the 1975 title track that named a whole late-night radio format. Who said it? Be honest. 🥧

Post on X
FacebookReveal

Yesterday's question: name a Smokey Robinson or Miracles song. Everybody said The Tracks of My Tears, Ooo Baby Baby and Cruisin'. Respect. The slick ones went Shop Around and You've Really Got a Hold on Me. Grandma's Favorite? Quiet Storm. It's the title track of Smokey's 1975 album A Quiet Storm, and it gave a whole radio format its name. In 1976 Melvin Lindsey, a Howard student, started a late-night slow jam show at WHUR in D.C., and Cathy Hughes picked Smokey's song as the theme. Late-night R&B radio still runs on it. Who said it? Be honest. 🥧

Post on X
FacebookReveal

Yesterday's question: name a Black woman chef. Everybody said Leah Chase, Carla Hall and Edna Lewis. Respect, Queen of Creole Cuisine got the crown. The slick ones went Nyesha Arrington and Tanya Holland. Grandma's Favorite? Lena Richard. In 1939 she put out her own cookbook in New Orleans, and a big publisher reissued it a year later. Then in October 1949 she got her own cooking show on WDSU, the city's first TV station, years before most folks had a TV in the house. Who said it? Be honest. 🥧
