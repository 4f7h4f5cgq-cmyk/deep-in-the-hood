Throwback Thursday. Bro got the floor tonight. Country music's first Black star played the harmonica. DeFord Bailey was one of the most popular performers on the early Grand Ole Opry in Nashville, starting in the 1920s, and his train song "Pan American Blues" was his calling card. In 1928 he was part of the first recording sessions ever held in Nashville. In 1941 the Opry let him go, and he spent years shining shoes and renting rooms in Nashville. In 2005 the Country Music Hall of Fame finally inducted him. We asked y'all for Black country artists last week. Who had DeFord? Be honest.

FacebookReveal

Yesterday's question: name a famous Black person with the last name Robinson. Everybody said Jackie Robinson, Smokey Robinson and Sugar Ray Robinson. Respect. The slick ones went Rachel Robinson and Craig Robinson. Grandma's Favorite? Sylvia Robinson. She sang "Love Is Strange" with Mickey & Sylvia in the '50s and had a solo hit with "Pillow Talk" in 1973. Then in 1979 she and her husband Joe put out "Rapper's Delight" on Sugar Hill Records, the record that took hip-hop off the block and onto the radio. They call her the Mother of Hip-Hop for a reason. Who said it? Be honest. 🥧
